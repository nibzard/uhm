# v0.6.3 — bound shell drainage and finish recovery retirement

v0.6.3 closes the two lifecycle issues deliberately deferred from v0.6.2:
redirected shell output can no longer hang past its deadline, and recovery
selection and cleanup now have deterministic, crash-safe semantics. It also
avoids probing ubiquitous command-line tools whose interfaces need no local
help capture.

## What changed

### Bounded child output and shared signal ownership

Redirected shell streams now use cancellable nonblocking readers and sinks.
Output can drain until the configured absolute deadline; a descendant that
keeps a pipe open past that point produces the bounded timeout result instead
of wedging a tee thread. stdout and stderr flag restoration is coordinated, so
aliased descriptors such as `2>&1` cannot leave the caller in nonblocking mode.

Shell and Python-program execution now share one process-wide signal owner.
Signals received before child activation are replayed once a target exists,
post-spawn errors kill and reap children before releasing ownership, and a
reaped PID or process-group number is never reused as a signal target during
output drainage. Forwarded interrupts retain their `128 + signal` result
instead of being reclassified as timeouts.

### Deterministic recovery selection and expiry

Every recovery capture receives an immutable sequence under the recovery lock.
`last` uses that sequence rather than directory iteration or later manifest
updates, and selection inspects the complete bounded inventory. If the active
manifest count exceeds `recovery.scan_limit`, selection fails closed and names
`uhm recovery prune --all` as the escape hatch.

Captures also persist an expiry deadline. Unpinned `available` or `conflicted`
evidence becomes logically unavailable at that deadline even before physical
garbage collection. Pinning must happen before expiry, suspends enforcement
without moving the deadline, and unpinning after the deadline makes the run
immediately unavailable again.

### Crash-safe pruning and finalization

Prune now persists non-restorable item intent before unlinking a snapshot.
Partially pruned runs report as `expiring`, are excluded from explicit restore
and `last`, and resume within the configured prune batch after interruption.
Whether a management-started cleanup requires a `RecoveryExpired` event is
sticky across automatic retries.

Management cleanup records one idempotent, directory-synced history event,
then acknowledges the terminal manifest and finalizes it. Completed restore
uses its already-durable completion event. Finalization durably removes the
snapshots directory before unlinking `recovery.json`, while unrelated history
artifacts in the run directory remain. Crash windows at every boundary are
retryable rather than leaving missing evidence or permanent tombstones.

A live recovery coordinator owns the recovery lock from capture through commit
or drop, renews its stale-process lease only after every preimage is durable,
and rereads and rehashes that evidence before the first output rename. A
concurrent prune therefore cannot retire evidence still owned by a running
program.

### Smaller tool-help surface

Common catalog tools and ubiquitous POSIX utilities such as `mv`, `grep`, and
`sed` are skipped before resolution. Naming one triggers no consent prompt, no
local help execution, no retained record, and no help bytes in provider
context; skipped names also do not consume the three-tool probe budget.

### CLI and documentation

`uhm recovery prune --all` is now documented as the explicit way to retire
otherwise-current inactive, unpinned evidence. Prune output reports finalized
manifest counts, and history-disabled cleanup leaves an unacknowledged expiry
pending instead of treating a no-op event append as deletion authority.

## Verification

`cargo fmt --all -- --check`, `cargo clippy --all-targets -- -D warnings`,
`cargo test --all-targets`, `python3 scripts/check-docs.py`, and
`git diff --check` all pass. The suite includes regressions for delayed and
descendant-held output, descriptor aliasing, signal handoff and cleanup,
selection overflow and ordering, logical expiry, partial-prune retries, event
and acknowledgment crash windows, live-coordinator ownership, evidence
revalidation, and finalization ordering.
